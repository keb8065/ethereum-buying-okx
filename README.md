## What are you actually buying: Ethereum or ETH?

Ethereum is the blockchain network. **ETH**, also called Ether, is the native cryptocurrency used on that network.

ETH has several functions:

- It can be held as a digital asset.
- It is used to pay transaction fees on Ethereum.
- It can be used in decentralized applications.
- It may be transferred to other people or wallets.
- It can be used in staking or other crypto services, depending on the platform and region.

The distinction matters because people often say they want to “buy Ethereum” when they mean they want to purchase ETH. An exchange order normally gives you ETH, not ownership of the Ethereum company or a traditional stock-like claim.

Ethereum.org describes ETH as the asset that powers the network, pays transaction fees, and supports activity across Ethereum applications.

The correct route depends on what you value.

The tradeoff is that an instant quote may be less transparent than a spot order. You should compare the total cash charged with the amount of ETH received rather than looking only for a line labeled “trading fee.”

Convert can make sense if you already have USDC, USDT, BTC, or another eligible asset and want to exchange it for ETH without placing a standard order-book trade.

- The amount of source crypto being used
- The amount of ETH you will receive
- The quoted conversion rate
- Any displayed fee or spread
- Whether the conversion uses the account or wallet balance you expected

Convert is convenient, but it does not necessarily produce the same result as placing a limit order on the spot market.

### Use spot trading if price control matters

Spot trading is more suitable when you want to:

- Place a limit order
- Compare available bids and asks
- Decide the maximum price you are willing to pay
- See the trading fee before submitting
- Trade repeatedly or manage larger orders

A **market order** is designed to execute immediately at available prices, but the final average price can differ from the last displayed price. A **limit order** lets you specify a price, but it may not fill, or it may fill only partially.

For a first purchase, a small test order is often easier to understand than jumping directly into a large trade. The goal is to confirm that you understand the account, balance, order type, and fee display before increasing the amount.

## How to store ETH after buying it

There are two broad choices: keep ETH on the exchange or move it to a self-custody wallet.

### Keeping ETH on OKX

Leaving ETH on OKX may be practical if you expect to trade, convert, or sell soon. It avoids an immediate withdrawal transaction and makes the asset easier to access from the exchange account.

The tradeoff is that you depend on the exchange’s account security, withdrawal rules, service availability, and access controls. Use a unique password, enable two-factor authentication, and treat every withdrawal request as a high-risk action.

### Moving ETH to a self-custody wallet

A self-custody wallet gives you control over the private keys. That control also means you are responsible for the recovery phrase, device security, wallet address, and transaction approval.

Ethereum.org emphasizes that self-custody places responsibility for securing assets on the wallet holder. Losing the recovery phrase or approving a malicious transaction can create problems that customer support may not be able to reverse.

For a first transfer:

1. Install the wallet from the official source.
2. Write down the recovery phrase offline.
3. Never share the phrase with anyone.
4. Copy the receiving address carefully.
5. Select the correct network on both sides.
6. Send a small test amount if the transfer is significant.
7. Confirm the transaction on the destination wallet.

Do not type a recovery phrase into a website, support chat, form, or message. A legitimate support agent should not need it.

## Common mistakes when buying Ethereum

### Confusing ETH with an Ethereum-based token

ETH and ERC-20 tokens are different assets. A wallet may support both, but they can have different contract addresses, transfer requirements, and network behavior.

### Ignoring the final quote

A “zero trading fee” claim does not automatically mean a zero-cost purchase. The exchange rate, spread, payment provider, card issuer, and withdrawal fee can still affect the result.

### Using leverage for a first purchase

Buying spot ETH means purchasing the asset. Futures, margin, and perpetual products introduce leverage, liquidation risk, funding costs, and more complex order behavior. A beginner searching for how to buy Ethereum usually wants spot exposure, not a leveraged derivative.

### Sending ETH on the wrong network

The asset name alone is not enough. Confirm the network supported by the receiving wallet. Ethereum mainnet and various layer-2 networks are not interchangeable in every wallet or service.

### Trusting a fake support account

Scammers often imitate exchanges, wallet providers, and account-recovery services. Never share a password, one-time code, private key, or recovery phrase. Do not approve a transaction merely because someone claims your funds are at risk.

### Buying because the price moved

A rising chart can create urgency, but the decision to buy should be based on the amount you can afford to lose and the purpose of holding ETH. A smaller recurring purchase may be easier to manage than one large order, but it does not remove market risk.
